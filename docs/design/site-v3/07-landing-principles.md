# 07 - Landing page principles for the Fee Insight homepage

> **Goal updated 2026-10-15 (James).** The site's goal is a Hamilton Pro subscription, monthly or
> annual. The paid report is a high-value lead, not the goal. The funnel: a free sample report, then
> the Competitive Fee Position Report ("from $300"), then Pro, either because the buyer wants it on a
> regular basis or because the report sparks a Pro demo request (through `/contact?source=demo`; no
> booking links). Where anything below ranks the report above Pro, this note wins.

Read date for all three sources: 2026-10-14. Each was read with WebFetch. Everything below is
paraphrased. Conversion figures are the sources' own claims. We have not checked them, and none of
them come from banking or fee-data products.

## GS - GoSquared, "The ultimate guide to designing a high-converting landing page"

**Principles.** GoSquared puts clarity ahead of clever design. Work out what you offer that nobody
else does, then say it plainly. The headline is the largest text on the page and may be the only
thing a visitor reads. The subheading supports it and leads toward the call to action. Customer
logos should appear early, and customer quotes should answer common objections. Repeat the CTA at
least once, because visitors skim. Write about benefits, not features. Keep navigation clean and
the footer free of distracting links, and use the footer for reassurance: founding year, customer
count, review scores. The guide says a homepage has to serve many audiences, so dedicated pages
should handle individual personas and products. Decide the page's single main goal and measure
conversions against visitors. A/B testing is useful, but GoSquared warns it is unreliable on
low-traffic sites.

**Examples.** The guide walks through about 55 pages, mostly SaaS. These are the ones most useful
to us:
- **Hotjar** states its value in three words.
- **Typeform** explains the product in five words set in the largest type.
- **Proof** offers two CTAs side by side: a demo or a signup.
- **ProfitWell** uses the CTA "get free metrics". The guide says this asks for less commitment than
  "try it free". It is an analytics product.
- **Baremetrics** and **Chargebee** build authority by publishing reports and webinars, a
  content-led approach that suits a data company.
- **DigitalOcean** puts pricing on the page so buyers have fewer steps before signing up.
- **Trulia** and **Airbnb** put a search box at the center, so visitors use the product before
  they are asked to sign up.
- **Revolut** uses an explainer video. **Pipedrive** uses video to keep visitors on the page, and
  color only on its main CTA. **RightMessage** offers a 3-minute demo video. **Cultured Code**
  makes watching its intro video the primary CTA.
- **Close** keeps the page monochrome so its one colored CTA stands out.

**Evidence.** GoSquared says its own research shows 96% of visitors leave most sites within 20
seconds. That is its only quantitative conversion claim. The guide does not discuss hero animation.

## UH - Unbounce, "15 High-Converting Landing Page Examples"

**Principles.** Unbounce defines "high-converting" as beating your category's average, and says no
single formula works everywhere. Its list:
- a hero image or video above the fold that shows the product being used;
- one visually obvious CTA, with competing links removed, including site navigation;
- a headline and subhead that state the benefit and what makes you different;
- benefits before features, plus social proof;
- message match between the ad or email that brought the visitor and the page;
- an ask that fits the incentive (do not request many details early);
- mobile-first design, including lighter mobile versions built for speed;
- CTA copy that says what happens after the click;
- continuous A/B and champion-challenger testing.

**Examples.** All 15 are consumer or small-business pages: Promo, edX, Simply Business, Later,
The Listings Lab, Twillory, TyresOnTheDrive, ooba, ClaimCompass, Extreme Lounging, onX,
Investing Shortcuts, MyTutor, College Board and FilterEasy. None is B2B data. The relevant
patterns:
- **Promo** puts a header video and explainer videos above the fold.
- **edX** found in testing that shorter copy did better.
- **Simply Business** breaks a complicated product into a few short steps and addresses cost and
  complexity worries directly. Its form only starts after the CTA, and it is multi-step.
- **Later** keeps the ad, email and page consistent.
- **Twillory** removes its video and GIFs from the mobile version.
- **onX** matches the visitor's search intent.
- **College Board** uses a real deadline.

**Evidence (Unbounce's claims).**
- The average landing page conversion rate is 6.6%, from its benchmark report covering more than
  57 million conversions.
- 83% of landing page visits are on mobile (2024 report).
- Video can lift conversions by "as much as 80%". No study is cited for this.
- The examples were picked because each converted above 30% on at least 500 visitors. Reported
  rates run from 30.02% (ClaimCompass) to 77.38% (College Board).

Unbounce suggests video lengths of a 30-120 second demo, a 60-second explainer or a 45-second
problem-solution hook. It does not say how animation affects conversion.

## UB - Unbounce, "The Best B2B Landing Page Examples That Convert"

**Principles.** The headline should name the goal the visitor is trying to reach rather than the
product. The page should make one offer with one CTA, show the product in use, put benefits first
with features underneath, and state a unique value proposition. Social proof can be logos,
testimonials, statistics or awards. Keep forms short now and collect more later in the sales
process. Name CTAs specifically ("Submit" sets no expectation). Serve several funnel stages: a
guide or webinar for early researchers, and a demo or trial for buyers who are ready. Segment
visitors by audience. Build competitor-comparison pages. Use visuals and video for complex
products and repeat the video's key points in text. Answer objections before they come up. Break
long pages into sections with anchor navigation.

**Examples.** The 28 examples are mostly SaaS. The ones closest to us:
- **Productive** uses outcome headlines, dashboard visuals and a "Book a Demo" CTA to make a
  complicated platform easy to grasp.
- **Thinkific** has an interactive earnings calculator that lets visitors model their own results.
- **Salesforce** uses bold statistics as its main proof.
- **MediaValet** follows a three-part structure: benefits, then stats, then a demo request.
- **HubSpot** routes each audience segment to a demo tailored to it.
- **Singular** pairs "Create Your Free Account" with "Talk to an Expert" for buyers at different
  stages.
- **Resource Guru** has a large play button for its explainer video and repeats the points in text.
- **Shopify** asks only for an email address.
- **Impraise** and **Reachdesk** gate a high-value guide.
- **Chargebee** and **Zoho** run pages that target competitors.
- **Blink** combines three kinds of proof: logos, testimonials and awards.
- **PaystubHero** uses compliance reassurance and trust badges.

**Evidence (the article's claims).**
- Changing three words in Going's CTA produced a 104% lift in an Unbounce A/B test.
- Salesforce's own figures for its Canadian customers: sales up 37%, satisfaction up 45%,
  marketing ROI up 43%.
- Visitors give a page under 15 seconds, citing HubSpot and Chartbeat data.

The article does not discuss hero animation.

## What the sources do not cover

None of the three tests a hero animation that plays on its own, and none looks at banking or
regulated-data buyers. The guidance on video is about explainer and demo videos that the visitor
chooses to play. The case for our 20-second sizzle has to come from those principles, adapted. Two
attention figures bear on its length: GS says most visitors leave within 20 seconds, and UB says
they give a page under 15. A sizzle that needs all 20 seconds before its point lands runs past
both. The rules below plan for that.

## Checklist for the Fee Insight homepage (data product, 20-second animated sizzle)

Each rule can be checked on the Vercel preview. The tags in brackets show which sources support it.

1. **The headline states the buyer's outcome, not the product.** It should be the largest text on
   the page, and someone who reads nothing else should still learn what they get: their fees
   compared with their market. [GS, UH, UB]
2. **The point is readable before the animation plays.** The headline, subhead and primary CTA are
   static HTML and appear in the first paint. If you pause the sizzle at 0 seconds, the page still
   explains itself. [GS, UB - the 15- and 20-second attention figures]
3. **The sizzle gives its key message by second 5.** The first frames show real product output (a
   fee comparison against peers), not a brand intro. A 0-5 second storyboard beat is written down.
   [UH "show it in use", UB "show the offer in use"; timing is our inference from GS and UB]
4. **Every claim the sizzle makes also appears as on-page text.** [UB - Resource Guru repeats video
   points in text]
5. **The sizzle shows the actual product.** It uses real `published_fee_catalog` output or a
   faithful render of it. No illustrative numbers, and every number on screen has a source
   (editorial policy). [UH, UB - Productive dashboards, Salesforce stats]
6. **The hero has one primary CTA and at most one secondary.** The primary leads toward Pro: "See
   where your fees stand" (the institution search), with "See Hamilton plans" as the secondary
   (03 section 4). The free sample report and the paid report are the lower-commitment paths, never
   the primary. [UH, UB - Singular; GS - Proof]
7. **CTA labels say what happens next.** For example, "Get your market's fee report", not "Submit"
   or "Learn more". [UH, UB - Going, B2B Quotes]
8. **The primary CTA appears again lower on the page.** At least twice in total, the second time
   after the proof section. [GS]
9. **The primary CTA is the only strongly colored button on the page.** [GS - Close, Pipedrive]
10. **There is proof near the hero, and it is specific.** Coverage counts (institutions, live fees),
    data freshness, and the accuracy measure. Only numbers we actually know; a gap is labeled as a
    gap. [GS, UB - Salesforce, MediaValet, Blink]
11. **Visitors can try the product before they commit.** A lookup of an institution or market, or a
    free sample, comes before any form. [GS - Trulia, ProfitWell "get free metrics"; UB -
    Thinkific calculator]
12. **The first form asks for very little.** Email plus institution at most. More detail is
    collected after purchase or at the demo. [UH, UB - Shopify]
13. **There are paths for visitors at earlier stages.** A free sample report or published research for
    people just researching, the paid report as the next step, and Pro or a Pro demo for people
    ready to buy. [UB funnel stages; GS -
    Baremetrics, Chargebee]
14. **Benefits come before features.** Each section opens with the decision it helps make (pricing,
    competitive position) and then explains how the data supports it. [GS, UH, UB]
15. **Objections are answered on the page.** Where the data comes from, how fresh it is, how it is
    checked, what Pro costs and what the paid report buys, in short FAQ or reassurance blocks. [GS, UB, UH - Simply Business]
16. **The navigation and footer don't pull visitors away.** Few header links, and footer items that
    reassure (who we are, method, contact). [GS, UH]
17. **Mobile gets a lighter hero.** At narrow widths, or with reduced-motion turned on, a still
    poster frame or short loop replaces the full sizzle. Largest contentful paint is measured on the
    preview. [UH - Twillory, 83% mobile claim]
18. **Persona pages carry the detail.** The homepage serves both banks and credit unions. Detail
    specific to one buyer goes on its own linked page. [GS, UB - HubSpot segmentation]
19. **Primary-CTA conversion is tracked from launch.** It is reported as conversions divided by
    visitors. We do not A/B test at our current traffic, and if we test later we change one thing
    at a time. [GS low-traffic caution, UH, UB]
