# Site v3 brief 01: market and buyer research (SHERLOCK)

Issue #1289. One-off brief for the landing page redesign, not a scheduled run.
Researched 2026-10-14 with web search and plain page fetches (no Firecrawl). Every competitor
claim below is what that company's own page or a named third party said on the date read; "not
observed" means not seen on the pages checked, not that the company lacks it. Third-party

## 1. The short version

- **No alternative found sells all three layers together.** Fee data vendors sell data (RateWatch,
  Market Rates Insight); peer platforms sell insight on call-report performance, not fee
  schedules (Callahan, America's Credit Unions, NAFCU, KBRA); consultants and program vendors sell
  the outcome (Moebs, StrategyCorps, Cornerstone, Velocity); consumer surveys sell a national
  average (Bankrate, MoneyRates). Fee Insight's pitch is data, insight and workspace in one place.
- **Nobody leads with provenance.** No homepage read says each figure traces to the institution's
  own published document. Datatrac says it collects by phone interview and website review; the
  others do not say how. Source-traceability is unclaimed ground.
- **Nobody shows a price for fee benchmarking.** Every B2B alternative read hides pricing behind
  "Request a Demo", a phone number or a free advisory session. The only public prices found are
  for adjacent products (AFP's commercial benchmark at $1,250/$1,450). Fee Insight publishing its
  report floor and Pro tiers is a real difference.
- **Buyer voice from fee owners is thin in public.** Trade press quotes executives and
  regulators about overdraft policy, not product managers about how they benchmark. What exists
  points to three pressures: competition for deposits, regulatory and political scrutiny of
  overdraft/NSF, and the need to justify fees in public. Treat the persona language in section 4
  as medium-to-low confidence until James has customer conversations.
- **Where alternatives are stronger:** years in market and brand (Moebs since at least 1999,
  Callahan "40 years", Datatrac "35+ years", RateWatch since 1989), consulting depth, outcome
  case studies (Curinos, StrategyCorps), named client logos (Moebs), free CU peer tools
  (Callahan CU Profiles), and commercial/treasury fee benchmarks we do not cover.

## 3. Regulatory and market pressure (primary or labeled sources)

- **The CFPB overdraft rule is gone.** The rule would have covered banks and credit unions with
  $10 billion or more in assets (a $5 cap, a cost-justified cap, or credit treatment). Congress
  disapproved it through S.J.Res. 18, signed as P.L. 119-10; the CFPB may not reissue a rule in
  "substantially the same form" without new law
  ([CRS IN12513](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2025-09-10_IN12513_eee84c24cdb66a1e947754dc87fc6c28c22c6c1a.html), 2025-09-10).
- **NCUA stopped publishing credit union overdraft/NSF income** from the March 31, 2025 call
  report cycle. Board member Otsuka dissented: "Transparency is vital for promoting fair
  competition" ([NCUA, March 2025](https://ncua.gov/newsroom/statement/2025/ncua-board-member-tanya-f-otsuka-statement-decision-remove-total-overdraft-and-non-sufficient-fund)).
  Implication for us: the public, regulator-published view of CU overdraft economics thinned;
  the published fee schedule remains a public record.
- **Pressure moved to states and advocates.** NCLC (an advocacy group; its figures, its method)
  estimates over $12 billion in 2025 overdraft/NSF fees, and urges states to cap overdraft fees
  at $5 and bar NSF fees ([NCLC issue brief, 2026-06-03](https://www.nclc.org/resources/overdraft-fees-rising-in-absence-of-cfpb-rule/)).
  NCLC draws fee amounts from published fee schedules, the same public source the Bank Fee Index
  reads. State fee-law content waits for James's legal review; nothing here is a legal reading.
- **Consumer survey averages are what the press quotes.** Bankrate's 2025 study: overdraft
  average $26.77, NSF $16.82 (their figures, 245 institutions, 2025-09-10). A July 2026 story
  quotes Moebs at $27 average for banks ([dnyuz, 2026-07-03](https://dnyuz.com/2026/07/03/why-some-banks-still-charge-high-overdraft-fees/)).
  These are the numbers a board member will have seen; a fee owner has to answer them with their
  own market.

What is **not** found in public: product managers describing their annual fee study, how long it
takes, what the board asks, or how they judge a fee dataset. Those phrasings in
`.agents/product-marketing.md` section 3 ("out of date by the time it reaches the board") remain
hypotheses, not sourced voice. The fastest fix is five short conversations with fee owners
(customer-research Mode 3); nothing in this brief replaces them.

How a fee owner is likely to judge fee data, inferred from what competitors choose to show and
what regulators and advocates argue about (inferred, medium-low confidence):

1. **Is it my market?** Callahan's own guidance and the ABA competition finding both point to
   "who are my real competitors", not a national average.
2. **Is it current?** Survey vendors publish annually or periodically (Bankrate's study ran over
   one month in 2025). FeeBuilder's pitch in 2015 was "daily updates", which shows buyers were
   told currency matters.
3. **Can I defend it?** Regulators, advocates and press now argue from published fee schedules
   and call reports. A figure that can be traced to the competitor's own disclosure survives that
   argument; an anonymous survey average is harder to put in front of a board.

Notes for the page: revenue is a real buyer value, but our editorial policy bars "maximize
revenue" and any advice to change a fee. The honest framing is "see what a price would mean
against your market", which is what Try a price does. The market layers in code are local,
state, district, peers (charter and asset size) and national (`src/lib/hamilton/research-layers.ts`);
the peer-group layer is worth naming on the page, since peer comparison is how the CU platforms
sell.

## 6. Gaps: where the three layers together are unmatched, and where we are weaker

**Unmatched, on the pages read:**

1. **Source-traceable fee data.** No alternative read claims each figure links to the
   institution's own published fee schedule. This is the clearest, most defensible headline.
2. **Line-by-line fee schedules in the buyer's own market.** Peer platforms compare call-report
   ratios; surveys sample large metros; FeeBuilder (2015) segmented by geography but its current
   status is unknown. Named local competitors, fee by fee, were not observed elsewhere.
3. **Data, aggregation and workspace in one product.** Data vendors stop at the dataset;
   consultants and program vendors start at the outcome. A self-serve workspace that moves from
   the figure to its market position to a board report was not observed.
4. **Public pricing and a free first look.** Free national and district reports plus published
   Pro tiers ($150 to $500 a month by asset size, `src/lib/pro-tiers.ts`) and a report from
   `REPORT_OFFER.priceLabel`. No fee-benchmarking competitor shows a price.
5. **Regulatory context inside the fee tool.** The Regulatory Wire sits next to the fee data; no
   competitor homepage pairs fee benchmarks with a regulatory feed.

**Where alternatives are stronger (be honest on the page and in comparisons):**

- **Track record and brand.** Decades in market and press citations (Moebs, Datatrac, Callahan,
  RateWatch). We have no customer logos or testimonials and must not imply any.
- **Outcome proof.** Curinos and StrategyCorps show client results; we have none yet.
- **Consulting depth.** Moebs's Pricing Institute, Cornerstone and StrategyCorps deliver advice
  and implementation. Hamilton is decision support by design and never tells an institution to
  change a fee; some buyers want the advice.
- **Coverage claims at scale.** Datatrac ("over 100,000 banking locations") and RateWatch
  (Marketplace listing: all institutions over $1B and 75% of the rest, per search summary) state
  broad coverage. Our coverage is live in `src/lib/public-stats.ts` and must be shown live, not
  quoted here.
- **Rates and commercial fees.** RateWatch and Datatrac cover deposit and loan rates; Curinos and
  AFP cover treasury management fees. The Bank Fee Index covers published consumer fee
  schedules only.
- **Free CU peer tools.** Callahan CU Profiles and association reports are free to CUs, which
  sets a price anchor of zero for "peer benchmarking" in the CU market. Our free national and
  district reports answer that anchor; the page should make them visible.
- **Measured accuracy.** We cannot claim an accuracy rate beyond what `checkFeeAgainstSource`
  has measured, and not "99%". Competitors make unqualified reliability claims ("most reliable
  benchmarking data"); we should not answer in kind.

## 7. Implications for the landing page (options, not decisions)

- Lead with what no one else claims: every fee traced to the institution's own published
  schedule, in the buyer's own market, current. Then show the three layers as one path: the
  figure, where it sits, what you do with it.
- Show the product's output on the page (a box-plot position, a source link) instead of a
  case-study number we do not have. Competitors ask for a demo before showing anything.
- Answer the national-average problem directly: the board has seen a survey average; the page
  can show the gap between a national average and a local market without naming a bank.
- Keep prices visible; it is a real difference in this category.
- Name the peer-group layer for CU buyers used to Callahan-style peers.
