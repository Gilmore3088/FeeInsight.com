# 2026-10-17: the database's log history grew with no limit
**What happened:** Supabase grew the disk from 8 GB to 12 GB. Measured that afternoon (read-only, by the Speed and efficacy audit thread), the database held 6.3 GB: about 3 GB of real data and about 2.5 GB of the pipeline's own history. `pipeline_attempts` was the largest table, at 1.35 GB and 2.3 million rows, all written since Oct 2: 1.67 million in the week of Oct 5 (backfill and re-checks) and about 110,000 a day after that. Its biggest writers were Darwin's re-checks (`verify.recheck` 315k rows, `verify.rules` 306k, `verify.category_model` 224k, `verify.peer_range` 103k), Hamilton's publish decisions (`publish.rules` 214k) and Knox's per-family "no candidates" rows (about 400k). The retired `agent_events` log still held about 420 MB in its April-August partitions.
**Cause:** nothing ever trimmed log tables. Every run appends attempt rows, run events and feedback, and many of them repeat the last row word for word. A time-only cutoff would remove almost nothing today: no attempt row was older than 30 days.
**Fix:** `atlas-storage-retention` (`src/lib/agents/atlas/storage-retention.ts`) measures the database every day, warns above 9,000 MB or 300 MB a day, and counts three trims: retired event-log rows over 60 days old, run events over 90 days old (keeping each step's newest event of every kind), and exact repeats in the attempt log's extract, verify and publish stages over 30 days old (the first and last copy of each run stay). Each deletes only after James's typed yes switches it on by PR.
**Lesson:** a table that every run appends to needs a retention rule in the same PR that creates it. Next: stop writing a row that repeats the previous one word for word at the source (the biggest writers above), which caps growth without any deletes.
