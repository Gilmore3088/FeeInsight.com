The quiet fees: small line items, wide spread
=============================================

Everyone benchmarks overdraft. Almost nobody benchmarks the fees a customer pays once a year, and those come up in a branch argument or a one-star review. The spread on these is often wider than on the headline fees.

Debit card replacement: low 25% → high 25%: $7.63–$43.75
Stop payment: low 25% → high 25%: $15–$30
Deposited item returned: low 25% → high 25%: $10–$25
Cashier's check: low 25% → high 25%: $3–$7.25
Bar length = the high-quarter price. Bank Fee Index, October 3, 2026.


THREE THINGS THE DATA SHOWS

1. Card replacement is the widest spread on the sheet. The median is $20, but the high quarter starts at $43.75. Credit unions (median $25) charge well above banks ($10). Check whether you're pricing a standard reissue like a rush order.
2. Returned deposits penalize the wrong person. The customer who deposited a bad check didn't write it. Median $15; credit unions run higher ($20).
3. Stop payment almost never drops below $15. Only 3.2% charge $5 or less. Banks sit at $30 vs $20 at credit unions. A lower online stop-payment price is an easy differentiator.


[ TERM WORTH KNOWING: HEADLINE FEE ]
Most schedules list several prices for the same fee (online vs branch, standard vs rush, per item vs per day). We benchmark the lowest published amount, the one a customer can actually get. If your lowest price is above the market's, no footnote will save the comparison.
